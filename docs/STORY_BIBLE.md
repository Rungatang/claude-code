# HOLLOW HOURS: story bible

Status: draft for review with the player (PLAN.md step 5, process item 1). Nothing in `js/story.js` has
been changed to match it yet. The working title stays HOLLOW HOURS until step 4b picks the one-word title;
where this document says "the title card", read whichever word wins.

The player's rules, all binding, restated so every section below can be checked against them:

1. Not a kids' game. Coercion, debt and death are the subject. No gore for its own sake.
2. Tam is confused for most of the game. His inner monologue is cut to short reactions.
3. Early on he learns only that someone wronged him and took his memory.
4. Everyone he meets blames the Regent.
5. He works out what the thread costs late-middle, and who he is only at the end.
6. Sef never lies outright in the first half. She leaves things out.
7. The story is told by the world (tablets, environment), by other characters who contradict each
   other, and by memory shards that are partial and sometimes not his.
8. The game grows from about one hour to four to six.

Conventions: "Act I" is the shore to the scripted loss, "Act II" is the underground to the Surface,
"Act III" is the Surface to the end. "Y-120" means a hundred and twenty years before the game starts.

---

## 1. Premise

A man wakes on a shore with no name and an hourglass fused into his chest, in a city that has run on
borrowed time for three hundred years: debts here are settled in hours, taken by hand at a dry well and
kept in glass. A woman called Sef gives him a name, Tam, a direction, up, and a reason, the Regent of
Hours, who sealed the city's hoard of hours inside the Great Clock a century ago and let everything
below it rot. The city agrees with her; everyone he meets has a grievance against the tower. He climbs,
takes powers from the city's guardians, and spends a thread he does not understand to use them. The
Regent beats him without effort, asks him a question he cannot answer, and drops him into the
underground, where the people who kept the ledgers, ground the husks and drew the maps tell him, one
contradiction at a time, that the kind voice at the well was never the Regent's. By the time he reaches
the light again the player knows what the thread is made of, and Tam is a few screens behind them. He
learns who he is in the last room: Sef's brother, hollowed by her hand and filled with four hundred
hours that four hundred people were asked for very kindly, built to walk through the Regent's wards and
open the Clock for her. What he does with the hours is the ending.

## 2. Cast

Each entry: want, secret, what they know about Tam, voice (two lines of actual dialogue), where they
appear and how their lines change by act.

### Tam

- Want: to know what the ticking is for. Later, to make the person who did it answer. At the end, to put
  the hours down.
- Secret (from himself): he was the Glassworks' best apprentice and he blew the vessel in his own chest
  himself, years before, for his sister, as a gift: "something that does not run out." He also gave her
  an hour once, voluntarily, to know what it felt like. He lived a long life on her hours before he burned
  her ledgers. He is not innocent; he is late.
- Knows about himself: nothing at the start. His name after the first boss. That he was wronged, by the
  end of Act I. The cost of the thread in the Garden. The rest in the Great Clock.
- Voice: nouns, questions, no explanations. He repeats what he has been told and gets corrected.
  - "Who."
  - "...That is a lot of names."
- Appears: everywhere. Act I: one-word reactions and questions. Act II: he quotes Sef and the lower city
  corrects him. Act III: he stops asking.

### Sef

- Want: the Great Clock open. Inside it is the city's reservoir of hours, enough to never run out. And,
  in her way, her brother back; she made him "last".
- Secret: among the four hundred hours she poured into him she added one of her own, from her own wrist,
  so that something in there was given rather than taken. She has never told anyone, and she tells
  herself that was the point of the whole thing.
- Knows about Tam: everything. She rehearsed his story to him on the shore.
- Voice: soft, practical, never raises her voice, says "we", asks permission for things she has already
  done.
  - "Sit a moment. You are shaking."
  - "I only ever took what was owed. Ask anyone who owed."
- Appears: the Jetty (A5), the Mouth (U4), the Great Clock (T3). New: a glimpse in the Grainmarket in Act
  I (the Pawnbroker: "Your friend was in. She did not buy."), and a letter in the Orphan Ward signed with
  one initial. Act I: gives a name, a seal and a direction, and lets the city do the blaming. Act II:
  gives Sever and a reason. Act III: tells the truth, because it is cheaper than lying.

### The Regent of Hours

- Want: to keep the Clock shut until he dies, and he cannot die until it opens. He would like to be
  allowed to stop.
- Secret: as a young clockmaster, two hundred and forty years ago, he signed the charter of the Debtors'
  Court. The Well was his guild's idea before it was hers. He sealed the Clock out of guilt as much as
  duty. He is not kind, and he never claims to be.
- Knows about Tam: smells the Well on him at once and knows what he is. Not who.
- Voice: tired, exact, irritable; "I do not have the hours" is literal when he says it.
  - "Who let you up the stairs?"
  - "I am very old, Tam. Let us see if I am still fast."
- Appears: Regent's Hall (T1, the loss), Throne of Hours (T2), the ending. The T1 tablet and the Court's
  charter speak for him before he does. Act I: dismisses Tam. Act III: fights him and tells him the one
  thing Sef left out.

### The Archivist (Hesper)

- Want: the ledgers back in order. To be forgiven for filing people.
- Secret: she opened the sluice that drowned the Archive, twelve years ago, on Sef's instruction and for
  payment in hours. She has lived down here since as a penance she set herself, and she blames the Regent
  out loud because it is easier than the other sentence.
- Knows about Tam: thinks he is one more debtor thrown down the oubliette. After the Index, that he
  "reeks of the Well" like every husk. Not the rest.
- Voice: dry, clerical, long sentences that end in a short knife.
  - "Most of them take a day to stop screaming."
  - "Mind the paper. It cuts."
- Appears: the Oubliette (D1). Act II: intro, again, after the Index. Act III: the end line and the last
  ledger page. Questline: the ledgers (section 6).

### The Miller

- Want: to see the sky. To stop the wheel. To not be asked about her.
- Secret: he is Sef's husband. He ran the Mills knowing what they ground. He has lived forty years past
  his span on hours she gave him unasked, which is how he knew she loved him and how he knew what it
  cost. He let the dog be taken.
- Knows about Tam: exactly who he is, on sight; he is Tam's brother-in-law. He says nothing until the
  Grinder is dead, and even then only "You have her eyes."
- Voice: flat, few words, farmer's humour.
  - "Do not mind the dust. It is only people."
  - "The wheel has stopped. I think I will go and see the sky."
- Appears: the Wheelhouse (M1). Act II: intro (now says "she" and "my wife" and nothing about a
  brother), again, after the Grinder. Act III: the ring and the word "brother" if the questline is done.

### The Pawnbroker (Odile)

- Want: a fair price. She hates owing and hates being owed, which is why she keeps a shop.
- Secret: she was the Hourwrights' assayer. She can tell whose an hour was by its weight on the tongue.
  The first time Tam pays her in grains she knows he carries hundreds of people, and she says nothing,
  because she does not discuss other customers. Sef is a customer; she still comes in to sell.
- Knows about Tam: what he carries. Not who, until he brings her something made of glass.
- Voice: short, commercial, dry; prices are her punctuation.
  - "Everything in here was somebody's. That is what a shop is."
  - "I buy hours. I do not ask where they were. Three grains."
- Appears: the Grainmarket in every act and the Surface in Act III. Act I: sells a map, a spool, a charm,
  and blames the tower in her own way ("He sealed the Clock. Everything since is interest."). Act II: buys
  hour fragments and names their owners. Act III: offers to buy the hourglass.

### The Cartographer (Wren)

- Want: a finished map of a city that rusts faster than she can draw it. She redraws Chain Street every
  month.
- Secret: she was mapping the tideline at dawn when Sef carried a man down to the water. She drew it,
  because she draws everything. She sells maps, not stories, so she has not said.
- Knows about Tam: that he was carried, and that "he did not have a face until I drew one in."
- Voice: precise, a little fussy, talks in bearings.
  - "Left is left. Up is where the money went."
  - "I do not draw what I am told. I draw what I walked."
- Appears: the Grainmarket; the Gallery (H6) in Act I, mapping the Cathedral; the Surface (U6) in Act
  III, where she gives Tam the page. Act I: sells region maps and blames the tower for the rust. Act II:
  absent (she is in the Ward counting beds for a chart). Act III: the page.

### The Rival (Corvin)

- Want: his mother's hour back. She gave it at his sickbed when he was six, went grey in a week, and he
  grew up in the Orphan Ward. The hour is inside Tam.
- Secret: he has sold most of his own hours to Odile to pay for the knife and the hunt. He has about a
  week left and knows it.
- Knows about Tam: that he is "the purse". He worked it out from the Ward's bed-ledger and a tablet in the
  Glassworks, and he has followed him since the market.
- Voice: fast, bitter, funny; calls Tam purse, bank, jar.
  - "You do your little trick and the glass gets lighter. You think that is free? Somebody paid. I know
    who."
  - "Four hundred and one, by my count. You are the one."
- Appears: the Grainmarket road (talks), the Glassworks (first fight), the Debtors' Court (second fight),
  the Garden (dying, talks), the Surface (the last choice). Act I: taunts. Act II: fights. Act III: asks.

### Mother Ilse (the Orphan Ward)

- Want: the children asleep. Sleeping children spend no hours.
- Secret: the Ward is Sef's charity. The children are taught to say "one hour is a small thing." Ilse was
  the first of them, ninety years ago. She has given eleven and has three left.
- Knows about Tam: assumes he is a messenger from "the Lady" because he carries her glass.
- Voice: lullaby cadence, gentle horror.
  - "Hush. They are counting in their sleep. Good children count."
  - "The Lady says an hour is such a small thing. She is right, you know. It is the smallness that gets
    you."
- Appears: the Ward's dormitory. Act I: welcomes him as the Lady's. Act III: asks him, if he comes back,
  whether the Lady is pleased.

### The Gardener (Ome)

- Want: to keep the unspent hours in the ground where no one can spend them.
- Secret: Ome is a husk who recovered. He gave everything at the Well, walked until he fell, and fell
  here, and one hour grew back. He is the only person in the city who ever got one back, and he knows the
  hours in Tam can be planted. He is the mechanism of the ending.
- Voice: slow, literal, kind.
  - "This one was a Tuesday. Nobody came for it."
  - "You can put them down here. Nothing here will ask for them."
- Appears: the Garden of Unspent Hours, late Act II; the Garden again in Act III. His lines change only
  with how many hours the player has returned.

### The Bailiff (the Debtors' Court)

- A construct of the Court, officer and boss. Speaks in the Court's formulas, never in the first person.
  - "The court finds. The court has always found."
  - "The defendant will state the hour."
- Knows about Tam: reads him as four hundred open cases and tries to serve them all.

### The Escapement, the Annealer, the Night Nurse, the First Debtor, the Unwound

Bosses with one line each; see section 7.

## 5. The twenty-four memory shards

Rules: a shard is shown as MEMORY with a title and two or three sentences; it never says whose it is.
Within a set, later shards complete or contradict earlier ones. Set III and IV contain memories that are
not Tam's at all; they are hours inside him, and the player works that out from the pronouns. The
existing eight are kept (marked "existing") and re-sequenced. Each set, when complete, unlocks a short
scene at a memory door (step 4's shard-gated doors), none of which gates a boss.

## 8. Endings

Keyed to two things: what Tam learns about Sef (set IV complete or not; the Miller's step 5 heard or
not) and what the player did with the hours (hour fragments returned to Ome in the Garden versus kept or
sold; Corvin given an hour or refused). Two endings are the base. The third is a branch on the same prompt
and can be cut without touching the other two.

The prompt, in the Great Clock after Sef falls, with the hands open:

> The glass is warm. Four hundred hours and one.
> GIVE THEM BACK / KEEP THEM / (third option if unlocked) SIT WITH HER

### Ending 1: PAID (default; always available)

As the current ending, with the narration cut to the bone. He empties the glass into the gears. The
hours go back to the city. He ends. The Regent buries the hourglass above the tideline. The stone reads
HE GAVE THEM BACK. If Corvin was given his hour, add one panel: a grave in the Ward with a cake on it.
If the player returned ten or more hours to Ome, Ome's garden is shown full; otherwise empty.

### Ending 2: KEPT (always available)

He closes the glass. He walks down through the Cathedral, the rust, the market. Odile's shutter is up;
she nods; "Fair." He walks past the shore and keeps going. Panel: the city's clocks running again, slow.
Panel: a man on a road at night, four hundred and one hours long, who does not look back, which is a
thing he learned from someone. Title card. If Odile bought the glass in Act III, replace the walk with
her epilogue: the hourglass on a shelf in the shop with a price on it; "Everything in here was
somebody's."

### Ending 3: THE RIM (optional; cuttable)

Unlocked only with set IV complete (he knows one hour was given) and the Miller's fifth step heard (he
knows she never asked the people she loved). Instead of fighting phase two, Tam spends every hour at once
on one Sever and stops time for the two of them. They sit on the rim of the dry Well in a moment that does
not end, her hand in his, nobody walking away slower. The city's clocks stay stopped. Panel: Ome,
planting the last flower, in a city where nothing wilts. Title card.

If the third ending is cut: the prompt has two options, the set IV scene still plays as lore, and the
Miller's fifth step still says "brother". Nothing else references the branch.

## 10. Open questions for the player

1. The repeated shore (shard 21, the Jetty scene): the game implies Sef has laid him on the sand more
   than once. It is the darkest idea here and it changes how the prologue reads. Keep it?
2. Endings: two (Paid, Kept) or three (plus The Rim)? The third needs set IV and the Miller's quest to
   be finishable in a normal run.
3. Tam made his own vessel (shards 6, 8, 24; the Annealer). It makes him complicit, not only a victim.
   Is that the Tam you want?
4. Corvin's hour: may the player give up one max-hour permanently? It is the only cost in the game that
   is the player's own, which is why it is there.
5. Who says "brother" first: the Miller (if his quest is done), the Regent, or only Sef? The draft lets
   all three, in that order, with Sef last.
6. The Garden sits between the Root's Marrow (U3) and the Mouth (U4), so Sef hands over Sever to a Tam
   who already knows the cost. The other placement is after the Mouth, which keeps her scene lighter and
   the reveal later. Which?
7. Does Sef appear in Act I beyond the Jetty? The draft gives her one offstage glimpse in the market and
   one letter in the Ward. More presence makes the twist heavier; absence makes it fairer.
8. The city's old name, ORIEL, is used only on the oldest tablets. Keep, change, or leave the city
   nameless?
